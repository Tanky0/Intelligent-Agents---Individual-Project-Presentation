# Evidence of development progress over the lifetime of the module

This account explains how the Unit 6 design became the individual Unit 11 implementation, the reasons for significant choices, and the problems found during development. The [README](../README.md) gives installation and execution instructions. [Comprehensive evidence of testing](testing.md) holds the test methods, results and their limitations. Test results are reported there.

The project began on 26 August 2026. A technical freeze baseline was recorded on 20 September, followed by documented corrections.

## Implemented system

A researcher supplies a question and inclusion and exclusion criteria. The Planning and Retrieval Agent uses the language model to create searches, runs them against PubMed or Semantic Scholar, and chooses whether to continue, refine, drop or finish. The graph applies the limits and records incomplete coverage. At the handover, deterministic code combines duplicate records, shares the screening allowance across the searches and checks publication status through Crossref once per distinct DOI.

The Curation Agent screens the selected papers in batches, producing a recommendation, relevance score, summary and supporting extract for each. Code checks whether the extract occurs in the paper's title or abstract. The researcher approves, flags or rejects the recommendation. Flagged or rejected papers can be sent back with a note, after which a fresh decision is required. SQLite stores only approved records that meet the identifier and extract checks. No run retrieves full text.

Planning and retrieval proceed autonomously from the supplied goal. Human approval is required before storage. In the terms Russell and Norvig (2022) use, the agent is goal-based and its environment is partly observed: it acts towards a stated goal, and what a search did not return is not visible to it, which is why coverage is reported rather than assumed.

## Design and implementation decisions

The theoretical links below explain the design's influence on the implementation. They do not claim that using a framework proves an agent architecture. Full references are in the [README](../README.md).

| Decision | Reason and implementation | Code and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hybrid planning, DC-01 and IN-01 | Following Wooldridge (2009), initial decomposition is separated from decisions made after retrieval. Here, "reaction at retrieval" means choosing the next move from the latest outcome. That operational reading does not establish a classical reactive layer of the kind Brooks (1991) describes, in which behaviour is produced without a symbolic world model: the next move here is chosen from a stored plan and a record of what was retrieved. | `agents/planner.py`, `graph.py`. AR-01 and IT-01 in [testing](testing.md). |
| BDI inspired state, DC-02 | Rao and Georgeff (1995) inform the distinction between the goal, intentions and information gathered. `ResearchState` carries the request, search queue and results. Keeping a queue avoids reconstructing the whole plan after every search. This is a conceptual influence, with no formal BDI interpreter, plan library or separate reconsideration procedure. | `schemas.py`. AR-01. |
| ReAct inspired loop, DC-03 | Yao et al. (2023) inform the action and observation cycle. A completed search leaves the queue. A refinement adds a replacement. The model supplies a short decision reason. A chain of thought is not requested. Deterministic routing applies stop conditions before following a move. | `graph.py`, `agents/planner.py`. IT-01, UT-23, AUD-02. |
| Two agents, DC-04 | Planning/retrieval and curation have distinct model responsibilities, informed by the discussion of multiple agents in Wang, Z. et al. (2025). Deduplication, ranking, selection, validation, reporting and storage remain ordinary Python or SQL. Giving those repeatable operations to the model would add uncertainty and cost. | `agents/`, `processing/`. AR-02. |
| Checked JSON handover, DC-05 | The proposal considered agent communication through the work of Finin et al. (1994). This application exchanges a small shared record. It does not use an agent communication language. LangGraph carries the state. A JSON buffer is written at the handover and checked again before curation. Whole state validation is explicit because LangGraph does not automatically perform every cross field check. | `schemas.py`, `graph.py`. IT-01 and IT-03. |
| Bounded recovery, DC-06 | Bratman et al. (1988) inform the use of bounded deliberation. A structurally invalid model answer permits three repair requests before escalation, subject to the run's remaining budget. Source retries and model service retries are separate mechanisms. An extract absent from its paper is marked as not found. Repeating the same string search cannot make it valid. | `agents/asking.py`, `llm.py`, `sources/http.py`, `processing/validation.py`. FT-04 and FT-05. |
| Evidence limits, DC-07 | The environment is only partly observed (Wooldridge, 2009). A paper records its text source and all searches that found it. Metadata only records are not described as abstract based evidence. The schema retains a full text field for compatibility. The current sources never populate it. | `schemas.py`, `processing/dedup.py`. UT-STRICT-01 and UT-28. |
| Human verification, DC-08 | The verification concerns discussed by Wang, G. et al. (2026) support a gate before storage. Approval is the researcher's decision. Storage additionally refuses records without a usable identifier or a found extract. Finding words does not establish that they support the summary scientifically. | `ui/app.py`, `processing/storage.py`. FT-06 to FT-09, SC-02 to SC-04. |
| Search reporting | The reporting principles in Page et al. (2021) and Rethlefsen et al. (2021) support recording methods, limits and departures. The application records actual searches, failures and records not screened. These reporting practices do not establish that the software alone produces a PRISMA compliant systematic review. | `processing/run_log.py`, `processing/audit_log.py`. FR-05 and FR-15. |

### Choices left open by the proposal

The proposal left these implementation decisions open.

| Identifier | Choice and reason |
|---|---|
| ID-01 | Python 3.13, with pinned dependencies, provided a supported environment for the selected libraries. The installation test is recorded in [testing](testing.md). A later package update requires a fresh check. |
| ID-02 | Optional years are sent using each search service's parameters and checked again on returned records. Out of range papers are excluded and counted. Papers without a usable year are retained and counted as uncertain coverage. Year filtering has automated tests. No live service check is recorded. The interface exposes a results limit of 10 to 1,000, default 50. The year fields are not displayed. Semantic Scholar applies its lower cap. |
| ID-03 | Deduplication tries DOI, PMID, exact title and then a RapidFuzz title score of at least 95. Group wide identifier conflicts prevent weak title evidence merging incompatible papers. Deterministic ordering and field tie breakers make the result repeatable. The threshold remains provisional: all merges in the 191 real records examined were resolved by identifiers, so that reading did not validate 95. |
| ID-04 | The English language scope is documented. A proposed language warning was not built. Nothing automatically establishes the language of every request or paper. This limitation was explicitly retained on 19 September. |
| ID-05, ID-06 | Source and model replies can be captured and replayed to support demonstrations during outages or exhausted allowance. All model access passes through `llm.py`, keeping model replacement, validation and request counting in one place. Capture does not confer permission to publish. |
| ID-07, ID-11 | Live retrieval and recall use the fixed screening question. Recall uses the fixed labelled sample. No new sample is drawn from the run's results. The public offline demonstration uses an explicitly invented question and papers. The measured recall used 1,000 requested results per search. The interface default is 50. Service caps and partial retrieval remain part of the reported result. |
| ID-08, ID-09 | The model scores relevance from 0 to 10 and can answer include, exclude or uncertain. Code ranks by score, then include before uncertain before exclude, preserving original order for ties. Uncertainty avoids forcing an unsupported binary answer. A revised paper keeps its place. The scale carries no anchors: the request names no meaning for any value, and no measurement calibrates one. The score orders papers rather than quantifying relevance. Both criteria are supplied with the papers, so a judgement records agreement with the criteria as written. |
| ID-10 | The JSON handover is also written to a local file so the buffer shown in the design exists as an inspectable artefact. It contains retrieved abstracts and is excluded from public release. A write failure is reported without inventing successful persistence. |
| ID-12 | Up to ten papers are screened per model request. Numbered answers are reconciled with the supplied papers, so changed answer order cannot attach a judgement to the wrong record. Batching reduces the number of model requests. Several papers share one prompt. |
| ID-13 | Capture, replay approval and publication are separate decisions. A manifest binds the relevant decisions to file fingerprints. Private replay permission is not a publication basis. Synthetic fixtures carry their own origin record. Promotion refuses incomplete approval. The operation is not atomic because the file and manifest are updated separately. |
| ID-14 | A merged record retains every source and query that found it. This preserves the search evidence after deduplication. |
| ID-15 | A run defaults to 18 counted model invocations and at most 50 papers for curation. Planning, movement decisions, repairs, service retries, revisions and replayed model answers use the same run budget. It does not enforce a shared daily allowance or count tokens. |
| ID-16 | After deduplication, screening places are shared round robin across searches. A paper found by several searches costs one place. Unclaimed papers are visited last. Those beyond the limit are retained in the coverage account. A smaller allowance than the number of searches cannot guarantee every search a place. |
| ID-17 | Storage prefers DOI, then PMID, then Semantic Scholar identifier, preserving aliases to avoid a later identifier creating a second row. It stores the judgement, summary and extract. The complete abstract and full text are excluded. A revoked approval removes the stored row. |
| ID-18 | A revision prompt receives a copy of the reviewer's note with prompt markers replaced and a 1,000 character limit. The complete note remains in the review record and log. These formatting controls do not prove that the model ignores an instruction embedded in prose. |
| ID-19 | Publication status uses three fixed classifications, with an empty value meaning no status was obtained. The most serious known status survives a merge. "Not checked" must remain distinguishable from "No retraction or formal concern identified". |
| ID-20 | Model captures carry a run identifier, including later revisions. Their filenames still depend on the model and prompt, so repeating the same request can replace an earlier capture. The identifier attributes a surviving capture. It does not preserve every previous response. |
| ID-21 | The review initially shows papers scored above 5 and counts those below the threshold. A paper already answered or revised remains visible. Hidden lower scored papers stay in the run and coverage record and are not stored automatically. The rule reduces the number of papers initially displayed. Relevant papers with lower scores may be hidden. The threshold rests on the uncalibrated scale of ID-08, which makes it a display convenience and not a measured cut-off. |

## Changes from the proposal

The tutor permits justified implementation changes arising from technical constraints, provided that the system retains goal driven planning, retrieval, processing and meaningful output. Departures from the original design are recorded below.

| Commitment | Implemented position and reason | Date and effect |
|---|---|---|
| PubMed Central full text, FR-08 | No run was connected to the source. It and its tests were removed because the implemented screening and extract check use titles and abstracts. The empty schema field remains. | 19 September. Full text retrieval is not implemented. This is a departure from the proposal. |
| Gemini 3.6 Flash, TC-01 | It remains the code's default. Seven live run attempts on the Flash models failed: six ended with 503 and one received no answer. Three had already performed searches. The successful run and later dataset runs used configured `gemini-3.5-flash-lite`. Both models are on the same free tier, and their published cards (Google DeepMind, 2026a, 2026b) give the context window and the intended use each was chosen against. | 18 September. The model was changed through configuration. The architecture was unchanged. Results name the model used. Comparative quality is limited to the reported samples. |
| pandas in processing, TC-06 | Deduplication uses validated records, lists and dictionaries with RapidFuzz. Dataframe conversions offered no needed operation. | 8 September. Streamlit retains pandas as a dependency. The application does not use it for deduplication. |
| Fifty abstract benchmark, SC-01 | The two samples contain 100 labelled records, 87 with abstracts. Records without abstracts were screened using the information available, with the source of those decisions documented. | Drawn 26 August. Screening on 26 and 27 August. The departure and small number of relevant papers qualify the recall result in [testing](testing.md). |
| Three two week sprints | The same broad order was fitted to the module timetable: sources and planning, curation/interface, integration and evaluation. | Scope freeze 13 September and technical baseline 20 September. Later corrections remain explicitly dated. |
| Additional evidence requested by the tutor | Class and sequence diagrams, plus standard dataset evaluation, were added after feedback. | Started 14 September, deliberately reopening the frozen scope for a recorded reason. |

The design made 37 commitments in all, of which 32 were kept and 18 delivered exactly as designed: the purpose of the agent and the researcher keeping the inclusion decision, taking a question and generating searches from it, the human verification gate before storage with every record carrying its extract, the exclusions on patient identifiable data and clinical recommendations, the out-of-scope list, the planning state as a conceptual model, the ReAct loop, the two cooperating agents with deduplication, validation and storage outside the model, the schema-validated buffer between them, the abstract-derived label on every record, the reporting of failed subgoals and coverage rather than silent degradation, three re-prompts before a plan escalates to a person, the review gate against a fabricated citation with coverage reported as the residual risk, Python with LangChain and LangGraph, Gemini 3.6 Flash on the free tier replaceable behind the orchestration layer, Pydantic for validation, SQLite and Streamlit, and VS Code with GitHub and pytest. The other fourteen kept commitments carry a qualification recorded here or in [testing](testing.md), and the departures are the table above.

The excluded uses remain patient identifiable data (EX-01), clinical recommendations (EX-02), literature in languages other than English (EX-03), paywalled full text (EX-04), citation graph exploration (EX-05), continuous monitoring (EX-06) and wider publisher coverage (EX-07). These define the scope. User input and retrieved text are not automatically checked for every excluded category.

## Limitations

Daily model requests across runs, token limits, incomplete retrieval, the provisional title threshold and the limits of extract matching remain disclosed limitations. They are properties of the evidence, not work left undone.

The interface observations come from one participant already familiar with the project. Phone, viewport, keyboard and theme checks not repeated in the second session, and changes not observed again, remain unverified. The observations are neither a System Usability Scale score nor a representative user study.
